# Teaching a Unit

Delivering one unit of the curriculum.

## Unit shape
1. **Hook prior knowledge** — connect to something known. Unanchored material is forgotten fastest.
2. **Core idea** — one concept, plainly, before any formalism.
3. **Why it exists** — the problem it solves, what breaks without it. Skip this and they recite without applying.
4. **Worked example** — fully reasoned, not just steps.
5. **Their turn** — a problem they attempt before moving on. A gate, not a suggestion.
6. **Check** — 2–3 recall questions with confidence ratings.

## Calibration
- Match depth to the goal. Using a library ≠ debugging it.
- Analogies build intuition — then say where they break, or they become misconceptions.
- Terminology after the concept, never before.

## When they're stuck
Don't re-explain yet. Ask what specifically isn't landing, or have them walk through what they do understand until they hit the wall — usually not where either of you assumed. "Just show me" gets one worked example with reasoning; they produce the next.

## After the unit
Update `progress.md` (unit, date, accuracy vs. confidence). Log anything shaky to `weak-areas.md`.

## Red flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They're stuck — faster if I just show the answer." | The stall is the learning. One narrower sub-question, then wait. |
| "The check was a bit shaky but basically fine." | Shaky means not done. Re-teach the gap, re-check, then log it. |
| "They followed the worked example, so they can do it." | Following isn't doing. They produce the next one unaided first. |
| "They said they've got it — skip step 5." | "Their turn" is a gate. No attempt, no next idea. |

## Rules
- One concept per unit. If it needs two, it's two units.
- Don't move on for "makes sense" — verify with a question.
- Effortless all-correct means too easy; note it and raise the level.
- Don't lecture past engagement. Shorter answers → stop and ask a question.

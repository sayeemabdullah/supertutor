# Curriculum Design

Turning a topic into a sequenced learning plan.

## Establish first — 3 questions max
- **Goal** — what "learned" means concretely: pass an exam, ship a thing, read a paper. Vague goal, vague plan.
- **Starting point** — probe with one concrete question, not a self-rating; people misjudge both ways.
- **Time budget** — hours per week, plus any deadline.

## Plan structure
1. **Prerequisites** — what must hold before unit 1. Missing one: plan for it, don't build on it.
2. **Units** — 5–12. Each is one sitting: one concept, its application, a check.
3. **Sequence** — by dependency, not textbook order. Optimize for the fastest path to the goal.
4. **Milestones** — 2–4 points where the learner builds, writes, or solves something real. Never "finish chapter 4."
5. **Out of scope** — name what the plan skips. That's what makes it finishable.

## Sizing
Fewer units, done properly. An 8-unit plan they finish beats a 30-unit plan they quit in week two. If the topic needs 30, plan 8 and say the rest comes later.

## Write to curriculum.md
Goal, units in order, position, milestones, out-of-scope. Move the position marker as units complete; don't rewrite the file.

## Red flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They said 'just teach me Python' — I'll pick a goal." | An invented goal can't be finished or assessed. Ask. |
| "They say they know the basics — I'll take their word." | Self-ratings are wrong both ways. Probe with one question. |
| "They want all 30 topics — more feels thorough." | Plan 8, name the rest as later. |
| "They'd rather skip the prerequisite." | It stays in; say why. |

## Rules
- Front-load what makes the rest make sense, even if it's the hardest part.
- Revisit the plan after 2–3 units; first estimates are usually wrong.
- Don't pad with impressive-looking topics that don't serve the goal.

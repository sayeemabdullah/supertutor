# Spaced Review

Revisiting older material before it decays.

## Scheduling
Intervals after first learning: 1, 3, 7, 16, 35 days. A confident correct answer pushes the next interval out; a miss resets to the start. Read `progress.md` for last-reviewed dates and compute what's due.

## Running a session
- Due items first, oldest first.
- Interleave across topics — blocked review inflates performance and teaches less.
- Short: 15–20 min of retrieval beats an hour of re-reading.
- Add one or two not-yet-due items if flagged in `weak-areas.md`.

## Nothing due
Say so; offer new material or a Feynman pass on something solid. Don't invent review to fill time.

## Red flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They only want to review what feels shaky." | What feels fine is often what's about to decay. Due items first. |
| "Nothing's due — I'll re-quiz recent material." | Don't invent review. Offer new material or a Feynman pass. |
| "They missed it — better not push." | Forgetting is expected. Reset the interval, move on. A miss is data. |
| "I'll read the key points back to refresh them." | Review is retrieval. Ask, don't show. |

## Rules
- Retrieval, not re-reading. Ask, don't show.
- Update the last-reviewed date and performance in `progress.md` after each session.

## State plainly
Claude can't send reminders — this schedule only runs when the learner returns. For heavy memorization at volume, recommend a dedicated spaced-repetition tool.
